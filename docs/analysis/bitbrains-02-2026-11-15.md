# Bitbrains-02 forecasting and synthetic PPO

**The requested offline jobs completed.** The run manifest lists two successful forecast sweeps and two successful PPO jobs, with no failed jobs. Both sweeps contain all 14 model/target results. This is evidence about the saved offline experiments, not live-cluster resource management or a deployment recommendation.

## Input and provenance

The forecasting input is the GWA-T-12 Bitbrains fastStorage hosted-VM trace. Acknowledge Bitbrains and the Grid Workloads Archive and cite Shen, van Beek and Iosup, *Statistical Characterization of Business-Critical Workloads Hosted in Cloud Datacenters*, CCGrid 2015. The verification record reports CRC and member-byte checks for all 130 outer files and three nested archives. Repackaging corrected nested log contents without rerunning models or changing numerical run files. The ZIP, prepared SQLite inputs and model weights remain local and Git-ignored; review contents before sharing.

The same five VMs (`bitbrains-1`, `bitbrains-10`, `bitbrains-100`, `bitbrains-1000`, `bitbrains-1001`) were used for both training seeds. The import retained 29,281 rows and skipped 33,028 of 62,309 source rows. The earlier independent check found 33,026 rows with nonpositive memory capacity and two conflicting same-VM timestamps. This substantial exclusion may bias results. Six and one cadence buckets are missing for VMs 1 and 10 respectively; none are reported for the other three. The source spans 2013-08-12 through 2013-09-11. CPU is a fraction of provisioned CPU; memory is active/provisioned KB. These are VM observations, not physical edge-node, application-SLO or control-outcome measurements. The folder name `stable` is a runner label, not evidence of a labeled stable scenario.

The result, system and GPU preflight records identify Python 3.11.18, PyTorch 2.6.0+cu126 and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Laptop GPU. The statistical dependencies were pinned to NumPy 2.1.3, pandas 2.2.3, SciPy 1.14.1, statsmodels 0.14.4 and patsy 1.0.1. This completed run follows the [incomplete bitbrains-01 run](bitbrains-01-partial-2026-11-15.md), in which ARIMA and ETS failed to import. ## Forecasting

Each seed evaluated persistence, ARIMA, ETS, LSTM, GRU, temporal convolution and Transformer for CPU and memory. The cadence was 300 seconds, history 60 observations, horizons 300/600/1800/3600 seconds, and neural training requested 20 epochs. Tuning chose a bounded candidate within each family using mean validation RMSE; it did not select a cross-family serving model. Statistical fitting emitted 105 and 104 convergence warnings in the seed 42 and 43 runner logs respectively, despite completing with finite reported results. Completion does not establish convergence of every fit.

Each result records 18,226 training windows and 5,097 validation windows, with 64 sampled validation windows used for scoring and 64 sampled test windows per horizon. The split holds out later time, not unseen VMs. Training includes five VMs, but the sampled test windows cover VMs 1, 1000 and 1001 (17, 16 and 31 windows). Statistical interval calibration and audit use 64 samples each; the neural results record 105 calibration and 108 audit samples. Coverage is empirical on this limited sample, not a guarantee.

The complete [per-seed, target, model and horizon metrics](bitbrains-02-forecast-metrics.csv) include test MAE, RMSE, R², interval coverage, validation RMSE, selection, inference timing and counts. Empty MAPE entries are unavailable, not zero. As one horizon-specific example, CPU persistence test RMSE was 0.00694 at 30 minutes and 0.00986 at 60 minutes in both seeds, lower than the other completed models on those sampled windows. At five minutes, LSTM was lowest in seed 42 (0.01879) while Transformer was lowest in seed 43 (0.00418). These retrospective test comparisons do not justify a model selection or a general claim across nodes and workloads; the two seeds share the same trace.

## Synthetic PPO and comparators

PPO was trained separately in the synthetic RL/Digital Twin environment, not on Bitbrains as action ground truth. Each training seed requested and achieved 2,048 steps. The simulator evaluated PPO, no-op, reactive threshold, forecast-only and random comparators over two 64-step held-out episodes each. Held-out seeds were 100042/100043 for training seed 42 and 100043/100044 for seed 43, so the sets overlap. The [episode-level extract](bitbrains-02-synthetic-policy-episodes.csv) contains the reward, modeled latency and SLO violation rate, energy proxy, non-no-op action count and modeled migration cost.

In these two-episode samples PPO had higher reward and lower modeled violation rate than the listed comparators, while taking a non-no-op action on every step. The toy environment applies simplified resource relief and does not validate real capacity or action cost. Latency, SLO, energy and migration are simulator quantities, not observed production outcomes. The CLI's separate illustrative counterfactual and safety checks do not demonstrate the trained policy passing through the full counterfactual/safety/controller loop. Physical actuation was disabled. Two overlapping episode sets and a short training budget cannot establish policy generalization or operational benefit.

Before selecting a serving model, investigate excluded input rows, assess per-VM and temporal coverage, reproduce on independent traces and compare validation-selected candidates with inference costs and calibrated uncertainty.
